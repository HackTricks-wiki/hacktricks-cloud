# Billing and Cost Management Pricing Calculator audit — 2026-10-01

## Result

Published the missing `bcm-pricing-calculator` inventory and two expected post-exploitation paths:

1. read saved workload/Bill estimates to recover planned services, account IDs, usage, costs, private rate results and commitment terms; and
2. on a management or standalone account, generate a Bill estimate that automatically incorporates the last consolidated anniversary bill without requiring Cost Explorer, CUR/Data Exports or general Billing read permissions.

No AWS defect was confirmed and no private report was created. A suspected workload historical-usage broker was specifically disproved: the API priced and stored the caller-supplied quantity instead of querying the referenced historical interval.

## Current control plane

- CLI/SDK client and IAM prefix: `bcm-pricing-calculator`; model version `2024-06-19`.
- Global endpoint: `bcm-pricing-calculator.us-east-1.api.aws`.
- CloudTrail source/Region: `bcm-pricing-calculator.amazonaws.com` in `us-east-1`.
- 36 operations cover workload estimates, Bill scenarios, Bill estimates, modeled usage/commitments, preferences and tags.
- Resource ARNs omit the Region: `workload-estimate/<uuid>`, `bill-scenario/<uuid>` and `bill-estimate/<uuid>`.
- Bill-estimate lifecycle events use EventBridge source `aws.bcm-pricing-calculator`.

## Live account and authorization results

The authorized account is an Organizations member. Administrator calls to `ListBillScenarios` and `ListBillEstimates` reached the service but returned `INVALID_REQUEST_FROM_MEMBER`, confirming that the management/standalone account gate is independent of IAM. Workload estimates were available and the original inventory was empty.

A disposable IAM user received only these calculator actions:

- `CreateWorkloadEstimate`
- `GetWorkloadEstimate`
- `CreateWorkloadEstimateUsage`
- `ListWorkloadEstimateUsage`
- `DeleteWorkloadEstimate`

Its policy explicitly denied `ce:*`, `billing:*`, `cur:*`, `bcm-data-exports:*`, `aws-portal:*` and `organizations:*`. A direct `ce:GetCostAndUsage` call failed with the expected explicit IAM deny, while calculator create/get/list/delete calls succeeded.

The workload test submitted an S3 Standard-storage line with quantity `1234` and a `historicalUsage` block pointing at the prior month. The create response and stored item both returned exactly `1234 GB-Mo` and the corresponding public-rate calculation. The service did not replace the quantity from real billing data. An invalid service/usage/operation tuple returned an item-level `BAD_REQUEST` and no usage item.

This establishes that calculator-only workload writes can act as a price oracle, but are not a historical Cost Explorer bypass. AWS's policy example correctly adds `ce:GetCostAndUsage`, `ce:GetDimensionValues`, `ce:GetCostCategories` and `ce:GetTags` for the console's historical import discovery.

## Bill-estimate disclosure boundary

Live creation was not possible because the account is a member. The current AWS API/user-guide contract establishes that:

- Bill estimates are management/standalone only;
- each estimate incorporates the most recent completed anniversary bill, existing usage/commitments, applicable discounts and sharing preferences;
- line-item results include payer/usage account IDs, original bill line ID/type, service/usage/operation/location/AZ, historical and estimated quantity/cost, and Savings Plan ARNs;
- commitment results include usage account, agreement/offering, Region, term, payment option and upfront/monthly payments; and
- AWS's documented Bill-estimate IAM example contains only `bcm-pricing-calculator:*` actions and no Cost Explorer permission.

This is classified as an expected cross-service permission-composition technique, not a vulnerability. `CreateBillEstimate` is itself a billing-data-bearing permission and defenders should treat it accordingly.

## Telemetry

Successful live calls were default CloudTrail management events. `CreateWorkloadEstimate`, `BatchCreateWorkloadEstimateUsage` and `DeleteWorkloadEstimate` had `readOnly:false`; `GetWorkloadEstimate` and `ListWorkloadEstimateUsage` had `readOnly:true`. Write events recorded detailed request parameters and create responses. Successful read events stored `responseElements:null`, so returned quantities/costs were not copied into CloudTrail.

Bill-estimate generation additionally emits best-effort direct EventBridge events for created/succeeded/failed states. AWS documents an email notification when generation completes. The combination makes fresh estimate generation especially noisy; reading an existing estimate avoids state, email and per-estimate charge but still produces CloudTrail reads.

## Cost and safety boundary

Workload estimates are free. Bill estimates have five free successful generations per calendar month; subsequent generations cost $2 each and can take 20 minutes to 12 hours. No Bill estimate was created in this audit. Future management-account validation requires explicit approval for the quota/charge and email/EventBridge side effects.

## Deferred validation

Retest in a disposable management or standalone account:

- create an empty Bill scenario and verify that a completed estimate exposes the baseline anniversary bill without modeled changes;
- prove the exact minimum create/read/delete resource policy and tag conditions;
- capture successful Bill-estimate CloudTrail request/response fields and lifecycle notification recipients;
- verify whether a member allowed `AFTER_DISCOUNTS` can infer negotiated per-unit prices through chosen workload lines; and
- test exact-resource isolation across unrelated saved workload and Bill estimates.

## Cleanup

All disposable workload estimates, IAM users, inline policies and access keys were deleted. Final matching `ht-bpc-*` IAM-user and workload-estimate inventories were empty. No Bill scenario, Bill estimate, Cost Explorer preference, Billing preference, CUR/Data Export, EventBridge rule, commitment or other account resource was created or changed.
